# Writing and ATS Guide

## Core narrative

The first 20–30% of the resume should answer:

1. What role is this person targeting?
2. What evidence proves they can do the core work?
3. What distinguishes their approach or domain context?

Use a focused target line and a short summary. Avoid an objective statement that only asks for a job; state the value the evidence supports.

## Bullet formula

Use one of these evidence-safe structures:

- `Action + context/scope + method + verified result`
- `Problem + action + delivery + validation`
- `Technical choice + implementation + issue resolved`

Strong verbs are appropriate when ownership is supported: `built`, `designed`, `implemented`, `integrated`, `diagnosed`, `automated`, `validated`, `deployed`, `reduced`. Do not upgrade `participated` to `led`, `assisted` to `owned`, or `prototype` to `production`.

Prefer specific nouns and methods over adjectives. Remove phrases such as “excellent communication,” “hard-working,” and “quick learner” unless followed by evidence.

## “Beautify” without fabrication

Acceptable:

- Reordering evidence by relevance.
- Replacing weak verbs with accurate stronger verbs.
- Turning a project description into problem–action–result.
- Removing repetition and generic claims.
- Clarifying architecture, trade-offs, validation, and collaboration.
- Giving a broad direction its own summary and section order.

Not acceptable:

- Adding revenue, percentage, latency, scale, user counts, team size, dates, titles, employers, or certificates.
- Claiming production use when only local testing occurred.
- Claiming independent ownership of collaborative work.
- Describing a course or plan as completed delivery.

## Summary pattern

Use two or three sentences:

```text
[Identity or strongest relevant background]. [Most relevant capability with evidence].
[Target-specific value or domain fit].
```

Example, fictional:

> Computer-vision graduate with two reproducible edge-inference projects. Built a camera-to-dashboard prototype using Python and ONNX, including data labeling, model conversion, and latency profiling. Seeking an applied CV role focused on deployment and field diagnostics.

## Section order

Consider:

1. Target line and summary.
2. Projects, internships, or work experience with the strongest match first.
3. Education and relevant coursework or research.
4. Skills organized by capability, not a wall of logos.
5. Certificates, awards, publications, or portfolio links.

Do not force this order when the evidence points elsewhere.

## ATS and layout

- Use one column and a standard reading order.
- Keep headings conventional: `Experience`, `Projects`, `Education`, `Skills`, `Awards`, or the local-language equivalents.
- Use selectable text and real headings.
- Avoid tables for core content, icon-only contact details, multi-column timelines, graphics containing important facts, and text inside images.
- Use no photograph by default. Follow local convention only when the user requests or the market clearly expects it.
- Keep dates in one consistent format. Put the most recent evidence first unless another order materially improves relevance.
- Use 10–12 pt body text, clear section hierarchy, and enough white space for scanning.
- One page is usually enough for early-career candidates; two pages are acceptable when the second page carries substantial, relevant evidence.

## Localization

- Chinese resumes often benefit from concise language, visible project evidence, and a direct target line.
- English resumes should use consistent tense: present for current work, past for completed work.
- Translate official titles only when the translation preserves meaning; keep the original in parentheses when useful.
- Do not invent an English version of an organization or award.
